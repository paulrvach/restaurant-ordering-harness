# Social visual identity

Version: 1.0 · Selected by the owner October 31, 2026.

## Reference and scope

Primary aesthetic reference: [A Day in HR on Motionimo](https://motionimo.xyz/video/a-day-in-hr). Original film: [A Day in HR on Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/1204518661), by MO MO / MO Creative Studio, with creative direction, design and animation credited to Or Drori and Maayan Erlich. Vimeo describes the Shapes brand film as a blend of UI, photography and motion design.

This is the default direction for our social images and short videos. Translate its visual language into restaurant operations using original photography, generated scenes, our own interface and our own copy. The guidelines below distinguish observed reference characteristics from our proposed production choices. This replaces the earlier food-editorial default.

## The direction

**A restaurant operator's real day, expressed through photography, software and playful collage.**

Make the work feel recognizable: a recipe, a crowded prep list, a vendor price sheet, a calculator, a notification, an order becoming clear. Mix human context with precise interface fragments. Keep the result contemporary, direct and a little playful.

## What we observed

- **Photography + UI:** everyday close-up imagery carries enlarged software layers. Around 00:17–00:18, a translucent calendar/event notification overlays a photographed desk scene. The poster frame uses a large context menu over a drink and laptop.
- **Operational graphics:** the opening sequence includes a tightly cropped spreadsheet, deep-green headers, mint row highlighting, thin rules and large black sans-serif text.
- **Time and rhythm:** time-of-day labels organize the workday; software interactions and changes of scale make mundane work visually active.
- **Cutout collage:** around 00:37–00:38, everyday objects, file/app symbols, message bubbles and UI fragments gather around a short, central word on a light field.
- **Typography:** clean sans-serif lettering, strong changes of scale, brief phrases and interface-like labels. The exact font family was not identified.
- **Palette:** Motionimo exposes the six colors listed below. They are reference swatches, not a verified official Shapes brand specification.

Source: direct visual inspection of the [film](https://vimeo.com/1204518661) and the [Motionimo palette](https://motionimo.xyz/video/a-day-in-hr), October 31, 2026. Times are approximate.

## Color system

These roles are our interpretation of the reference palette.

| Token | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Clean panels, negative space and clear UI surfaces |
| Work green | `#1E7246` | Main brand accent, table headers and selected actions |
| Mint highlight | `#C6F1D8` | Selected rows, calm blocks and resolved states |
| Neutral gray | `#919794` | Secondary surfaces and rules; not small text on white |
| Muted sage | `#5E7267` | Supporting tones, subdued photographic accents |
| Slate blue | `#3F4B62` | Occasional secondary accent |
| Ink — proposed addition | `#101713` | Legible body copy and outlines on light surfaces |

Use mostly light or photographic space, one strong green move, and restrained secondary color. UI semantics may introduce another color when it conveys real meaning. Ensure readable contrast; do not apply a swatch to text just because it appears in the palette.

## Typography and copy

- Proposed working font: **Inter**, with Arial/system sans-serif as fallback. This is our choice, not a claim about the film's typeface.
- Use one short, large headline, usually 3–8 words. Sentence case; regular or medium weight for UI labels, medium or semibold for the main thought.
- Pair a prominent phrase with smaller, practical UI text. Keep essential numbers, units and prices clean and aligned.
- Write in the operator's language: “One recipe. Vendor-ready.”, “Packs, not guesses.”, “What do we actually need?” Treat these as optional original headline examples, not measured claims.
- Prefer specific progress over hype. Any performance, accuracy or time-saving number needs evidence.

## Composition recipes

### 1. The workday moment

Start with an authentic or clearly illustrative restaurant scene: hands reviewing a recipe, a prep counter, a laptop beside a supplier sheet, or a phone near receiving boxes. Add one oversized UI element: a quantity edit, a vendor selection, a question or a source-price row. Give the UI a clear visual purpose and a crisp edge against the photo. A small time-of-day label can anchor the story.

### 2. The operational close-up

Fill most of the frame with a clean, cropped recipe/order table. Use a green header, a mint selected row, thin dividers, large sans-serif labels and generous row spacing. Highlight one calculation or decision. Use exact synthetic values or real, permitted evidence; label illustrative UI as a concept.

### 3. The collage payoff

Use a light central field with a short headline. Arrange 3–6 relevant cutouts around it: ingredient, recipe card, vendor pack, order row, calculator, small notification or cursor. Give objects clear silhouettes and enough space to read at feed size. Keep the center calm. Use our own assets rather than lifting the film's branded objects or UI.

## Photography and image generation

Favor candid work surfaces, believable light, tactile objects, close crops and human presence through hands or gestures. Balance those organic elements with precise digital overlays. Avoid making every scene an immaculate food advertisement. The central subject is the ordering work, rather than a plated dish.

For generated scenes, create the photographic or collage base first. Add exact prices, quantities, labels and final typography with deterministic layout tools when precision matters. A generated interface is concept art, not evidence that the app works. For completed milestones, use a genuine screenshot inside the same visual system.

Use [IMAGE-PROMPT.md](IMAGE-PROMPT.md) as the shared starting brief. Record the final prompt, generation method and selected asset inside each post folder. Supply meaningful alt text.

## Motion — proposed production rules

For future clips, adapt the reference's lively workday rhythm: photographic moment → UI action → order/result. Use purposeful crops, quick pushes, match cuts, cursor movement and cards entering/exiting. Tie every movement to a change in the task.

Suggested starting timings: 0.2–0.4s UI transitions and 1.5–3s readable headline holds, adjusted to the actual content. These are our proposed values, not measured timings from the reference. Keep captions usable with sound off and provide a still or reduced-motion alternative where appropriate.

## Social production defaults

- Landscape: 1600×900 or 1536×1024; square variant: 1080×1080 when useful. These are house defaults, not platform limits.
- Keep key text comfortably inside the image edges; check the actual feed crop before posting.
- One post, one visual idea, one primary headline, one evidence point.
- Use green/mint and the photo/UI relationship consistently, without forcing every image into the same layout.
- Final review: recognizable restaurant task, readable headline, supported claim, honest screenshot/concept label, accurate alt text, and no exposed private data.

## Avoid

Rustic cookbook styling as the default; decorative serif headlines; a warm beige still life with no software/work context; generic neon AI imagery; dense dashboards reduced to unreadable thumbnails; excessive glass effects; decorative arrows without meaning; fake metrics; copied Shapes/MO MO branding.
